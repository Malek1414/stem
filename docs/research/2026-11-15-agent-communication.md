# Efficient communication between LLM agents: literature 2024-2026 and what to build for the operator<->coder link

Date: 2026-11-15. Method: every arXiv entry below was opened through the arXiv API (title, authors, date, abstract confirmed). Numbers marked "abstract" come from the abstract; numbers marked "html" come from the paper HTML; "repo" means checked with `gh api`. Anything I could not confirm is tagged **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## 0. Setup facts that decide most of the answer

- Operator model: `Qwen/Qwen3-Coder-Next` is architecture `Qwen3NextForCausalLM` (config.json, opened): 48 layers, 512 experts, 10 active per token, `full_attention_interval: 4`, 2 KV heads, head_dim 256, hidden size 2048, Apache-2.0 (HF API). So only every 4th layer (12 of 48) is full attention with a normal KV cache. The other 36 are linear-attention (Gated DeltaNet) layers with a recurrent state, not a KV cache. Every KV-sharing paper below assumes plain attention KV in all layers. None tested a hybrid or MoE model. Porting them is research work, not config. (This is my reading of the config; no paper addresses it.)
- Computed from config: KV for the 12 attention layers is about 24.6 KB per token in bf16 (12 layers x 2 x 2 heads x 256 x 2 B), so a 10k-token context is about 250 MB. Size is not the problem, model support is.
- Ollama: its API doc (`docs/api.md` in ollama/ollama, headings read) lists generate/chat/embed/structured outputs, and the only state handle is the deprecated `context` token array. There is no endpoint for hidden states or KV export/import. I found no open Ollama issue for it (two `gh search issues` queries, nothing relevant). Any latent method needs a different backend.
- Other backends: llama.cpp `llama-server` has `--slot-save-path` and `POST /slots/{id}?action=save` (server README, opened), but that only saves and restores one model's own prompt cache. It is not cross-model. vLLM has `docs/features/prompt_embeds.md`, `disagg_prefill.md`, `kv_offloading_usage.md` (file listing), and LMCache (Apache-2.0, 12.4k stars, repo) as KV connector. transformers exposes `past_key_values` and hidden states, which is what the research code uses.
- So: text-level methods run on Ollama today. Latent methods need transformers or vLLM, and per-pair training for some of them.

Reading across the table (my synthesis):
1. The gains over good text are modest (about 3-5 points accuracy). The big wins are latency (2.5-11x) and tokens, and they come from skipping the sender's decode. Our messages are short task cards, so the text decode we would save is small (a card of 150-300 tokens at the operator's decode speed is about a second or two). The larger handoff cost is the coder re-prefilling the shared context, and that is solved by prefix caching (KVCOMM, llama.cpp `cache_prompt`, vLLM prefix cache), not by latent messaging.
2. All benchmarks are reasoning/QA/ALFWorld with 0.5-14B dense models. Nothing was tested on tool calling, computer use, code editing sessions, or MoE/hybrid models.
3. Same-weights-family sender/receiver is the realistic case: operator and coder from one family would let you share KV for the same context (DroidSpeak/KVCOMM idea) when both are fine-tunes of the same base. Different bases need a trained fuser (C2C, Interlat, XBridge).
4. Closed models (the user's Claude, other people's agents) cannot take part in any latent scheme. The text link must stand alone anyway.
5. Latent messages cannot be logged, read or debugged by a human, which conflicts with the "shared live workspace as fallback" requirement.

Takeaways for text: (a) measure first; for 2 agents, topology pruning papers give nothing. (b) Compression wins are mostly "send less history, send a delta, reuse prefix". (c) No paper I found measures operator-to-coder handoff latency for computer-use or coding agents. **No evidence exists for our exact case**; the numbers above are from QA/reasoning/web tasks.

## 3. Training agents to communicate; is 712 examples enough

Evidence:
- Optima (2410.08115) trains communication directly with generated-and-ranked data, an automatic reward with a token penalty, and iterates; it did not use a few hundred human examples.
- LIMA (Zhou et al. 2023, arXiv 2305.11206, opened): 1,000 curated examples were enough for a 65B model to "learn to follow specific response formats from only a handful of examples". It is the standard evidence that format and style transfer needs little data.
- AgentTuning (Zeng et al. 2023, arXiv 2310.12823, title confirmed, abstract not read) mixes agent trajectories with general data to keep generality; relevant for avoiding overfitting the handoff format.
- Multiagent Finetuning (Subramaniam, Du, Tenenbaum, Torralba 2025, arXiv 2501.05707): specialise copies of one base model on data from their own interactions; shows that role-specific fine-tuning plus self-generated data keeps improving across rounds.
- Latent methods have a much larger training appetite: C2C fuser 500k samples (116-step variants on 15k), Interlat full ALFWorld trajectories with three losses.

My assessment (judgement, not a cited result):
- 712 handoffs are enough for a LoRA/SFT that teaches the card schema, terse progress lines, and when to hand off. This is a format-and-policy task, in the LIMA regime. Hold out about 70 for evaluation, because with 712 a few repetitive session types can dominate.
- They are not enough for any latent fuser, and are the wrong data type for it (a fuser is trained on generic text pairs, not on handoffs).
- To go beyond SFT, build a verifier from the done-criteria (tests pass, file exists) and run Optima-style rejection sampling: sample handoffs, keep the shortest ones where the coder succeeded, retrain. That grows 712 into a few thousand with a token-penalised reward.
- Risk: the examples come from real sessions where the sender was probably a frontier model. Check that cards in the data are consistent in schema; if they are not, normalise first, otherwise you teach the inconsistency.

## 4. When multi-agent splits help or hurt

- **Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?** Cemri, Pan, Yang, Agrawal et al. 2025. arXiv 2503.13657. 1,600+ annotated traces, 7 frameworks, 14 failure modes in 3 groups (system design, inter-agent misalignment, task verification). Failure rates across systems 41%-86.7% (html). Most frequent modes: step repetition 15.7%, reasoning-action mismatch 13.2%, unaware of termination 12.4%, disobeying task spec 11.8%. Prompt/role fixes gave +9.4% (ChatDev, giving a role final say on termination) and a verification step +15.6% on ProgramDev. Implication: explicit done-criteria and a verifier belong in the card.
- **Towards a Science of Scaling Agent Systems.** Kim, Gu, Park, Park et al. 2025. arXiv 2512.08296. 260 configurations, 6 benchmarks, 5 architectures. Abstract: multi-agent gains range from +80.8% (decomposable finance) to -70.0% (sequential planning); tool-heavy tasks incur multi-agent overhead; coordination returns diminish once the single-agent baseline is strong; architectures with centralised verification propagate errors less. Computer-use and tool calling are the tool-heavy, sequential case, so expect the split to cost, not help, unless the coder work is separable.
- **Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems on Multi-Hop Reasoning Under Equal Thinking Token Budgets.** Tran, Kiela. 2026. arXiv 2604.02460. Under matched reasoning tokens, a single agent matches or beats multi-agent on multi-hop reasoning across Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1-Distill, Gemini 2.5; reported MAS gains are often unaccounted compute. Qwen3 included, so applicable to our family.
- **When Single-Agent with Skills Replace Multi-Agent Systems and When They Fail.** Li. 2026. arXiv 2601.04748. Compiling a MAS into one agent with skills cuts tokens and latency at similar accuracy in preliminary tests; skill selection collapses past a critical library size. Preliminary results.
- **Should we be going MAD?** Smit, Duckworth, Grinsztajn et al. 2023. arXiv 2311.17371 (title confirmed; older, not read). Multi-agent debate does not reliably beat single-agent baselines.
- **Cognition, "Don't Build Multi-Agents"** (2025-06-12, via search result) and **"Multi-Agents: What's Actually Working"** (Walden Yan, 2026-04-22, page opened). The later post: what works is multiple agents contributing judgment while writes stay single-threaded (reviewer with fresh context; a smaller primary model forking full context to a bigger "smart friend"); parallel-writer swarms remain "mostly a distraction"; managers over-specify and agents do not share state well. They say a small primary model (SWE-1.5) paired with Sonnet 4.5 only partly worked.

Implication for operator/coder: a split pays off when (1) the operator would otherwise burn its context on long coding loops, (2) the coder runs with a clean, focused context, and (3) one agent owns writes. It hurts when handoffs lose context or when the task is a tight tool-use loop. Measure with a single-agent baseline (operator alone on the same tasks) before trusting the split.

- Interlat license conflict (GitHub NOASSERTION vs README Apache-2.0).

- The Ollama "no hidden states/KV" statement rests on the API doc headings and a grep of `api.md`, plus the absence of issues; not tested live. I did not touch the DGX Spark.

## 6. Ranked recommendation for our setup

| Rank | Item | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Typed task-card JSON (goal, constraints, context, done-criteria) enforced with Ollama structured outputs, plus a free-text `notes` field | Implement now | Cheap, works on Ollama and with closed Claudes; the Routed Graph Handoff result says structure saves 2-3x tokens but needs a free-text escape and a receiver that knows the schema. |
| 2 | Prefix/prompt cache reuse for the shared context (keep a stable prefix, send deltas, `cache_prompt`) | Implement now | Removes the re-prefill cost that dominates a handoff; KVCOMM reports TTFT 430 -> 55 ms in a harder setting, and it needs no new model access. |
| 3 | Progress streaming as short typed events (`started`, `blocked`, `done` plus 1 line) and an append-only shared log/workspace | Implement now | Low latency, human-readable, doubles as the fallback channel and as future training data (ESAA-style). |
| 4 | Explicit done-criteria plus a verifier step in every card | Implement now | MAST: verification step gave +15.6% on ProgramDev; verification failures are a main failure group. |
| 5 | Single-agent baseline benchmark on your own tasks | Implement now | Scaling-agents and equal-budget papers show splits can lose up to 70%; tool-heavy sequential work is the risky case. |
| 6 | SFT/LoRA on the 712 handoffs for schema adherence and terse messages (hold out about 70) | Implement after training | LIMA-regime data size is enough for format; needs the coder base chosen and schema normalised first. |
| 7 | Optima-style loop: sample handoffs, keep the shortest successful ones, retrain with a token-penalised reward | Implement after training | Only method shown to train for fewer tokens with maintained quality (under 10% tokens); needs an automatic success check. |
| 8 | Shared-KV between operator and coder if both are fine-tunes of one base (DroidSpeak/KVCOMM style) | Implement after training | Only worthwhile on vLLM/transformers, and the hybrid Qwen3-Next layers are unsupported by the papers; revisit after the coder base is chosen. |
| 9 | C2C / Interlat / XBridge fuser between different bases | Not worth it now | Per-pair training on 100k+ samples, no tool-use evidence, white-box only, small models only; gain over good text about 3-5 points. |
| 10 | LatentMAS-style latent thoughts | Not worth it now | Training-free but same-model, reasoning benchmarks only, no audit trail, no Ollama; our messages are short. |
| 11 | Topology search (AgentPrune, AgentDropout, G-Designer) | Not worth it | They prune graphs of 4+ agents; a two-node link has nothing to prune. |
| 12 | Custom compressed dialect between our agents and other people's Claudes | Not worth it | 2609.06129 shows compression can cost more (parse failures, +8-11% tokens) and needs both sides to agree; closed models cannot join latent schemes at all. |
